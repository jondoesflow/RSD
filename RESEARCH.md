# RSD research notes

These are the sources behind the content in Steady, with the main points taken from each. Steady supports understanding between partners. It is not medical advice.

## Core articles

### ThinkADHD: ADHD and Rejection
https://thinkadhd.co.uk/adhd-and/adhd-and-rejection-sensitivity-dysphoria-rsd/

- RSD means intense emotional reactions to rejection, criticism, exclusion or judgement, whether real or only perceived. It is not a separate diagnosis, but it is common with ADHD.
- Small things can trigger it, such as a casual comment or a minor critique. People often respond by withdrawing or by avoiding risk.
- In relationships, neutral comments can feel like attacks. That leads to misunderstandings, outbursts or withdrawal. **Telling loved ones about RSD helps build understanding and patience.**
- What helps: self-awareness (recognising that "this is RSD"), self-compassion, reframing, support from people you trust, CBT, coaching, support groups, and small achievable goals that build resilience.
- RSD is "part of how your brain responds… not a reflection of your worth."

### ADDitude: Dr William Dodson on RSD
https://www.additudemag.com/rejection-sensitive-dysphoria-and-adhd/

- *Dysphoria* is Greek for "difficult to bear."
- **Internalised** RSD can look like a sudden major mood disorder, sometimes including suicidal thoughts.
- **Externalised** RSD shows up as an instant, intense rage at whoever or whatever seemed to cause the pain.
- Onset is abrupt and recovery can take a while.
- Common coping patterns are people-pleasing, avoidance, and perfectionism or overachieving.
- Dodson reports that alpha agonists (guanfacine or clonidine) and MAOIs help some people. Any change like this needs to go through a clinician.

### Simply Psychology: Rejection Sensitivity in ADHD Relationships
https://www.simplypsychology.org/rejection-sensitive-dysphoria-relationships.html

- **Say:** name what you see without judging it, validate before you explain, use "I" statements, be specific about neutral events, and offer a clear next step.
- **Avoid:** "always" and "never", vague silences, "you're overreacting", fixing things too quickly, reassuring until you are drained, and pressure.
- **Signs of an episode:** a neutral event treated as rejection, assuming you are angry or leaving, intense anger or tears, self-critical talk, withdrawal, sudden people-pleasing.
- Episodes can last hours or even days.
- **Repair:** wait until you are both calm, open gently, share your side without blame, make a plan together, agree a pause signal, then reconnect.

### Gottman Institute: time-outs
- When either partner is flooded, take a break of at least 20 minutes and then come back. The return is what makes it a time-out rather than a walk-out.

  https://podbay.fm/p/adhd-chatter/e/1735603233

  https://podbay.fm/p/adhd-chatter/e/1766444400
- An episode with Rich Forbes, an ADHD coach and ex-soldier, includes a segment on "How to Calm Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria".
